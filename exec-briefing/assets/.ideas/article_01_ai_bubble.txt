THE AI BUBBLE IS LEAKING — WHAT SMART LEADERS SHOULD DO BEFORE IT BURSTS

The AI spending frenzy is running into hard reality. Leaders who act now on
value realization — not hype — will be the ones still standing when the
correction comes.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE INSIGHT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The signals are unmistakable. In February and March 2026, Wall Street began
what analysts are calling a "Great Rotation" — institutional capital pulling
out of high-flying AI-adjacent tech stocks and migrating into old-economy
cyclicals. The Shiller PE ratio hit 38.33 in January 2026, a level historically
associated with market peaks. Microsoft, one of the most aggressive AI
investors, fell over 20% year-to-date by late March as markets questioned
whether its AI spend would yield promised returns fast enough.

But this is not a technology problem — it is a value-realization problem. MIT
Sloan's Thomas Davenport and Randy Bean put it squarely: the AI boom mirrors
the dot-com cycle in its overemphasis on user growth over profits. Their 2026
prediction is a "level-set year" — the bubble will likely start deflating, and
probably sooner rather than later. Meanwhile, Forrester's Chief Research
Officer has been direct: "In 2026, the AI hype period ends as the pressure to
deliver real, measurable results intensifies."

The deeper issue for enterprise leaders is this: a 2025 survey found that 95%
of enterprise users reported low ROI on early GenAI investments. Separately,
McKinsey's State of AI report shows that while over 75% of organizations now
deploy AI in at least one function, only 31% of prioritized use cases have
reached full production. The gap between adoption and enterprise value is not
a future risk. It is already the present reality.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  • Audit before you expand. Before approving the next AI investment cycle,
    map your current AI portfolio to measurable business outcomes. If you
    cannot isolate AI's contribution from surrounding system improvements,
    your ROI case will not survive board-level scrutiny — and it should not.

  • Shift from individual to enterprise value. MIT Sloan's Davenport and Bean
    are clear: organizations that moved GenAI into enterprise workflows —
    rather than leaving it as a personal productivity tool — are the ones
    building compoundable, measurable value. Every AI initiative that sits
    outside a shared data pipeline is a stranded asset.

  • Demand measurable ROI within 90 days. Analysts from Forrester and
    TechTarget are aligned: if a vendor or internal AI project cannot show
    hours saved, errors prevented, or revenue generated within 90 days,
    treat it as a warning sign — not a use case needing more time.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PRACTITIONER'S TAKE
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The bubble will not burst for every organization equally. The ones that
  hurt the most will be those that confused activity for strategy — running
  pilots, deploying Copilot, and calling it AI transformation. The ones
  that emerge stronger are already doing something different: they are
  treating AI as an infrastructure investment, not a feature rollout.
  This is not the time to slow down on AI. It is the time to get precise.
